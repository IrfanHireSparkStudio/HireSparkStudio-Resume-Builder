For fewer mistakes

  1. Speak at a steady, natural pace — not word by word. Dictation understands full sentences far better than single words.
  2. Say your punctuation out loud — it converts automatically: “full stop” → ., “comma” → ,, “question mark” → ?, “exclamation mark” → !, “semicolon” → ;, “colon” → :, “dash”, “apostrophe”, “percent”, “at sign”, “open / close bracket”, “open / close quote”, “new line”, “new paragraph”. One-word forms like “fullstop” and “questionmark” work too. Urdu mein bhi: “فل اسٹاپ” → ۔, “کاما” → ،, “سوالیہ نشان” → ؟, “فجائیہ نشان” → !. Keep “Voice punctuation” ON (above) for this, and the first letter of every new sentence is capitalised for you.
  3. Keep the room quiet and the phone close — about a hand's length from your mouth. Background TV or fans are the biggest cause of wrong words.
  4. Names and rare words may still come out wrong. Fix them once in the editor, then use Copy or Download — your corrected text is what gets saved.
  5. Long session? Leave “Keep listening” on and the mic restarts by itself after short pauses, so you can dictate a whole document in one go.

On a laptop, Chrome and Edge give the best results. On Android, use Chrome; on iPhone, dictation works in Safari and Chrome on recent iOS versions.
